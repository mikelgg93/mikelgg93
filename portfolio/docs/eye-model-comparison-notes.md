# Part V — prescriptions and population sampling

English and Spanish articles compare the Parts I–IV teaching assembly with explicit versions of the Escudero-Sanz/Navarro (1999) and centred Atchison (2006) models. Three demos progress from surface geometry to one normal variable to a correlated five-variable population. No external illustrations or photographs were added. The banner is generated by `scripts/build-eye-models-banner.ts` from the same geometry and seeded samples.

## Source and definition audit

- **Escudero-Sanz & Navarro (1999), DOI 10.1364/JOSAA.16.001881, Table 1:** corneal radii 7.72/6.50 mm, Q −0.26/0; CCT 0.55 mm; internal ACD 3.05 mm; lens radii 10.20/−6.00 mm, Q −3.1316/−1; LT 4.00 mm; VCD 16.3203 mm; image radius −12 mm. Distances sum to 23.9203 mm. This version has a homogeneous equivalent-index lens. Do not silently replace it with another Navarro version.
- **Atchison (2006), DOI 10.1016/j.visres.2006.01.004:** select the centred version, not its decentered/tilted companion. Geometry and the six SR relations were checked against the author's review with Thibos (2016), DOI 10.1111/cxo.12352, Table 1. Fixed dimensions: CCT 0.55, internal ACD 3.15, LT 1.44 + 2.16 = 3.60 mm. VCD = 16.28 − 0.299 SR; anterior corneal R = 7.77 + 0.022 SR. Retinal Rx = −12.91 − 0.094 SR, Ry = −12.72 + 0.004 SR, Qx = 0.27 + 0.026 SR, Qy = 0.25 + 0.017 SR. Lens radii 11.48/−5.90 mm and Q −5/−2 are also reproduced in the optical prescription of the primary study *Influence of material and haptic…* ([institutional full text](https://zaguan.unizar.es/record/117601/files/texto_completo.pdf)). The demo uses −10…0 D and does not add accommodation or implement the GRIN ray equations.
- **Our assembly:** internal ACD is derived from shared Part IV corneal/lens positions, giving 3.52 mm. The lens patches exactly match its ellipsoidal halves, using R = a²/b and Q = a²/b² − 1. The comparison shows the smooth RPE envelope at 24.30 mm, not the anterior neural surface/foveal pit. Published image-surface endpoints and measured axial-length reference conventions are explicitly distinguished.
- **Rozema, Atchison & Tassignon (2011), DOI 10.1167/iovs.10-6705, Appendix Tables A1/A2:** extract right-eye columns 2, 13, 14, 12 and 15 (one-based), in the order K, CCT, internal ACD, LT, AL. Means and every covariance entry were cross-checked against the authors' full text. Units are D followed by four mm entries. This is a marginal of the assembled statistical model, whose lens data and biometric data came from different datasets; it is not a new clinical cohort or the paper's full refraction-filtered output. The selected 5 × 5 submatrix is positive definite. No complex-valued matrix workaround is copied from the original larger-model appendix.
- **Rozema et al., SyntEyes (2016), DOI 10.1167/iovs.15-18067:** author/institutional full text, [Zaragoza repository](https://zaguan.unizar.es/record/48368/files/texto_completo.pdf). Used for the conceptual move to reduced corneal-shape variables and a mixture of two multivariate Gaussians. Its model is not implemented. Its ACD convention starts at the corneal epithelium; its numbers must not be mixed into the internal-ACD subset without conversion.

K is conventional keratometric power: R(mm) = 337.5/K(D), using index 1.3375. It is not physical two-surface corneal power, and the transformed radius is not exactly Gaussian. The population renderer holds Q, posterior corneal shape, lens surface curvatures and posterior surface shape fixed. It does not infer a person's refraction from AL.

## Numerical and graphics boundaries

The three comparison views share camera scale and fixed drawing apertures (cornea Ø10, lens Ø8.4, posterior patch Ø18 mm). Surfaces are open patches with illustrative opaque colours. The biconic equation uses signed optical radii and converts posterior propagation to Three.js −z. The existing teaching lens is reproduced exactly within its displayed aperture. There is no whole-eye optical solver, optical-quality ranking or clinical population validation.

The statistical path is deterministic Mulberry32 → open-interval uniform numbers → Box–Muller normals → Cholesky transform. Independent mode zeros off-diagonal covariance entries while preserving target means/variances. Both modes start with the same random stream; later rejection can change which candidate indices survive. Mean and covariance units/order are included in the downloadable JSON.

Whole-vector rejection applies broad, explicitly illustrative drawing guards; component-wise clamping is avoided. VCD is derived by axial closure. The attempt budget is bounded. Normal-histogram draws remain unbounded and include all tails; σ = 0 is displayed as a point mass without evaluating a singular density. The gold curve is density scaled by sample count and bin width, not an exact finite-bin integral. All scatter coordinates come from accepted finite vectors.

Five patch buffers are updated in place. Scene creation, on-demand animation, resizing, context restoration and disposal use the established shared scene helper. Download object URLs and timers are revoked/cleared, including on unmount. Part V does not modify the Neon integration or the existing article/demo implementations.
